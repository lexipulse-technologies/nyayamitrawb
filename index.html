Frequently Asked Civil Legal Questions (India & West Bengal)

How is online land mutation applied in West Bengal through Banglarbhumi?
Under Section 50 of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, 1955, rural and district land mutation is applied online via the Banglarbhumi portal using Form 13 with the registered Sale Deed, up-to-date Khajna receipt, and BL&LRO hearing. In Kolkata municipal wards, property assessment mutation is handled by the Kolkata Municipal Corporation (KMC) under the KMC Act, 1980.
What is the procedure to evict a tenant in West Bengal under WBPT Act 1997?
Residential and commercial premises eviction in West Bengal is governed by Section 6 of the West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997 (or Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 where applicable). The landlord must serve a valid statutory Notice to Quit expiring with the end of the tenancy month, followed by an eviction suit before the Civil Judge or Presidency Small Causes Court in Kolkata.
What are the rules for cheque bounce under Section 138 NI Act in India and Kolkata?
Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, the payee must send a statutory 15-day written demand notice within 30 days of receiving the bank cheque return memo. If the drawer fails to pay within 15 days of receipt, a complaint may be filed within 30 days before the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate (Bankshall Court in Kolkata) or Judicial Magistrate.
How to claim maintenance under Section 144 BNSS 2023 in West Bengal and India?
Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 (replacing Section 125 CrPC) entitles spouses, dependent children, and elderly parents to monthly maintenance. Applications with an Affidavit of Disclosure of Assets and Liabilities (per Rajnesh v. Neha) are filed before the Family Court or Magistrate.
How can homebuyers file a builder delay complaint under WBRERA or RERA India?
Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (WBRERA in West Bengal), homebuyers can file Form M for delay compensation or refund with SBI MCLR + 2% interest, and seek execution under Section 40 before the District Magistrate.
How does ancestral property partition and succession work in India and West Bengal?
Ancestral and joint property partition in West Bengal follows the Dayabhaga / Hindu Succession Act framework and Order XX Rule 18 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, seeking preliminary decree of shares and final decree by metes and bounds. Non-testamentary inheritance uses Legal Heir / Warishan certificates, while wills require Probate or Letters of Administration under the Indian Succession Act, 1925.
